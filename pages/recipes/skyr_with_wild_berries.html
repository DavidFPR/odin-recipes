Skyr with Wild Berries

Ingredients

  • 2 cups skyr (Icelandic yogurt)
  • 1 cup wild berries (such as blueberries, lingonberries, or cloudberries)
  • 2 tbsp honey or maple syrup
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract (optional)
  • 1 tbsp chia seeds (optional, for added texture)

Directions

  1. In a bowl, mix the skyr with honey or maple syrup. Add vanilla extract if using.
  2. Top the skyr with wild berries. If using frozen berries, thaw them slightly before adding.
  3. Sprinkle with chia seeds if desired.
  4. Serve immediately, or chill in the refrigerator for 30 minutes for a colder treat.
  5. Enjoy as a healthy breakfast, snack, or dessert!

A Little Bit of History...

Skyr is a traditional Icelandic dairy product that dates back to the Viking Age. Made from strained yogurt, it has been a staple in Icelandic cuisine for over a thousand years. Unlike regular yogurt, skyr has a thick, creamy texture and a tangy flavor. Its popularity among the Vikings was due to its rich nutrient profile and its ability to be preserved for long periods, making it ideal for harsh Icelandic winters.

The practice of consuming skyr with wild berries is deeply rooted in Icelandic tradition. Wild berries, such as lingonberries and cloudberries, were foraged by the Vikings and have been enjoyed with skyr for centuries. These berries not only add a burst of flavor but also provide essential vitamins and antioxidants. Combining skyr with these berries reflects the Vikings’ deep connection to their natural surroundings and their use of local resources.

Today, skyr remains a beloved part of Icelandic cuisine, celebrated for its health benefits and cultural significance. The addition of wild berries makes this dish not only delicious but also a nod to ancient Viking dietary practices. Enjoying skyr with berries is a way to connect with the past and savor the flavors of Iceland’s wild landscapes.

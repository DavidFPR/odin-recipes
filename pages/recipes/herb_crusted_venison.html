Herb Crusted Venison

Ingredients

  • 2 venison steaks (about 6 oz each)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil or animal fat
  • 1 tbsp fresh rosemary, chopped
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme, chopped
  • 1 tbsp fresh parsley, chopped
  • 2 cloves garlic, minced
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 cup breadcrumbs (optional for extra crust)

Directions

  1. Preheat the oven to 200°C (400°F).
  2. In a small bowl, combine rosemary, thyme, parsley, garlic, salt, and pepper.
  3. Rub the venison steaks with olive oil or animal fat, then coat evenly with the herb mixture. For a thicker crust, press the breadcrumbs onto the steaks as well.
  4. Heat a skillet over medium-high heat and sear the venison steaks for 2-3 minutes on each side, until a golden crust forms.
  5. Transfer the steaks to a baking dish and roast in the preheated oven for 8-10 minutes, or until the desired doneness is reached.
  6. Let the steaks rest for a few minutes before slicing and serving.

A Little Bit of History...

Herb crusted venison was a cherished dish among the Norse people, who valued game meats for their rich flavor and nutritional benefits. Venison was a key part of the Viking diet, especially in regions where hunting was a common practice. The use of herbs like rosemary and thyme reflected the Vikings' connection to their natural surroundings and their ability to utilize local flora to enhance their dishes.

In Viking society, hunting was not only a means of obtaining food but also a significant cultural activity. Successful hunts were celebrated, and the meat from these hunts was often prepared with care and respect. The herb crust used in this dish was a way to honor the bounty of the hunt and add a touch of sophistication to what was often a hearty and rustic meal.

The tradition of seasoning meats with herbs and spices continued through the ages, evolving with new culinary techniques and ingredients. For the Vikings, this practice was a reflection of their resourcefulness and their deep respect for nature. Herb crusted venison remains a testament to their culinary ingenuity, combining the simplicity of wild game with the elegance of fresh herbs.

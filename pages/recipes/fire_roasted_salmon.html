Fire Roasted Salmon

Ingredients

  • 1 whole salmon, gutted and cleaned (about 2-3 lbs)
  • 2 tbsp olive oil or animal fat
  • 1 tsp sea salt
  • 1 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp fresh dill or dried thyme
  • 1 lemon, sliced
  • 4-6 wooden skewers or a grill grate

Directions

  1. Prepare a campfire or grill with medium heat. If using skewers, soak them in water for 30 minutes to prevent burning.
  2. Rub the salmon inside and out with olive oil or animal fat. Season generously with sea salt, black pepper, and herbs.
  3. Place lemon slices inside the cavity of the salmon. If using skewers, thread them through the fish lengthwise to hold the body in place.
  4. Roast the salmon over the fire, either directly on a grill grate or suspended by the skewers. Cook for 15-20 minutes per side, or until the flesh is opaque and flakes easily.
  5. Remove from heat, let cool slightly, and serve hot!

A Little Bit of History...

Fire-roasted salmon was a beloved dish among Viking seafarers and settlers who lived along the coastlines of Scandinavia. With the ocean providing an abundance of fish, salmon was a prized catch, offering both rich flavor and vital nutrients. Cooking over an open flame was a natural method for the Vikings, who often built fires along the shores after a long day of fishing or raiding.

The Viking connection to the sea ran deep, and salmon, with its high-fat content, was a perfect source of energy for their grueling voyages. Roasting the fish over an open fire gave it a smoky flavor, while the addition of herbs like dill or thyme added a taste of the wild Scandinavian landscape. Lemon, though not native to the region, was later introduced through trade and became a complementary ingredient in Viking cuisine.

Fire-roasted salmon was often prepared during communal gatherings or celebratory feasts, where the Vikings would share their spoils with family and friends. For the Vikings, food was more than just sustenance; it was a way to honor their connection to the land and sea, and to celebrate their victories. This dish, simple in its preparation but rich in flavor, remains a testament to the resourcefulness and ingenuity of the Norse people.

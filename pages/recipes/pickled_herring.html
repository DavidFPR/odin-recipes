Pickled Herring

Ingredients

  • 4 whole herring, cleaned and filleted
  • 1 cup white vinegar
  • 1 cup water
  • 1/2 cup sugar
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp whole black peppercorns
  • 1 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 bay leaves
  • 1 tbsp fresh dill, chopped

Directions

  1. In a saucepan, combine the vinegar, water, sugar, and salt. Bring to a boil, stirring until the sugar dissolves.
  2. Add the peppercorns, mustard seeds, and bay leaves to the vinegar mixture. Remove from heat and let it cool.
  3. Place the herring fillets in a glass jar or airtight container. Layer with sliced onion and dill.
  4. Pour the cooled vinegar mixture over the herring, ensuring the fish is fully submerged.
  5. Seal the jar or container and refrigerate for at least 3 days before serving. The longer it sits, the more flavorful it becomes.
  6. Serve chilled as an appetizer or as part of a traditional Scandinavian meal.

A Little Bit of History...

Pickled herring has been a staple in Scandinavian cuisine for centuries, and its origins can be traced back to the Viking Age. The preservation of fish through pickling was a crucial technique for Vikings, who needed to ensure a steady food supply during long winters and sea voyages. Herring, being abundant in the North Atlantic, was a perfect candidate for pickling.

The practice of pickling not only extended the shelf life of the fish but also added a unique tangy flavor that complemented the otherwise mild taste of herring. Pickled herring became a vital part of Viking food culture, often featured in feasts and shared among communities. It symbolized both the ingenuity of the Vikings in food preservation and their deep connection to the sea.

Today, pickled herring remains a beloved traditional dish in Scandinavia, celebrated for its historical significance and distinctive taste. It serves as a delicious reminder of the resourcefulness of the Vikings and their enduring culinary traditions. Enjoying pickled herring is not just about savoring a dish but also about connecting with the rich heritage of Viking gastronomy.
